# BRIC-A-BRAWL: read this first

You are building a browser party fighter for 2 to 8 friends who join from one link. This page is the whole project in miniature. The detail is in `MASTER_DESIGN.md` (chapters 1 to 12) and `ART_BIBLE.md`. When this page and those disagree, they win; tell us.

## The game

Glazed ceramic ornaments fight on a floating dinner plate that cracks and falls apart under them. Percent damage, launch people off the edge to win (Smash). Real frame data, guard, a five-frame parry, grabs, combos, three motion inputs (Street Fighter). Household junk falls from the sky on doilies and exists to shove people: bellows, a plunger on a string, a champagne cork (the silly guns). The island is made of chunks with hit points; it breaks on a schedule and breaks faster when fighters are slammed into it (the physics). Rounds last two minutes. Four fighters: **Earl** the teapot, **Grog** the basalt bull, **Nesta** the nesting doll who sheds shells, **Moss** the gnome with a fishing rod.

## The five pillars. Every decision serves one

1. **The ten-second rule.** Click to hitting a friend in ten seconds. The lobby is playable before the network connects.
2. **Everything chips.** Damage shows as cracks on fighters; the island is damaged by where people land; the arena is the timer.
3. **Shove, don't stab.** Position is the only health bar. Gadgets do almost no damage. Guard does not stop you sliding off.
4. **Honest frames.** Fixed, published frame data. Nothing is plus on block. Latency is handled by rules a player can learn.
5. **One link.** No install, no account, no server.

## Constraints you may not design around

- Build machine: 2 vCPUs, 8 GB RAM, 19 GB disk, no GPU. One heavy job at a time (Blender, browser, full tests), enforced with `flock`.
- No API keys, accounts, paid services or servers. Static site on GitHub Pages.
- All models come from headless Blender scripts, exported as glTF. Any third-party file must be CC0, MIT, Apache, BSD or OFL and listed in the licence manifest.
- 60 fps on a laptop with integrated graphics, eight fighters on screen. Playable within ten seconds.
- **Banned:** neon, glow, bloom, synthwave, cyberpunk; voxels and cube-and-primitive geometry; Kenney, Quaternius, KayKit, Synty, Mixamo, chibi packs and their look-alikes; anything that reads as a generic jam game.

## The architecture in six sentences

The simulation is one TypeScript package with no DOM, no three.js and no clocks; it advances in fixed 60 Hz ticks from button masks and emits events. The host's browser runs it in a Web Worker and is the only authority. Other players send 16-byte input packets at 60 Hz and receive snapshots at 30 Hz; each predicts only its own fighter and interpolates everything else 66 ms behind. A hit is tested against where the attacker saw the target; guard and parry are tested against what the defender is doing now, with three ticks of grace. Rendering, effects and audio read sim state and events and never write to it. The same package runs headless in Node for bot matches and for an eight-peer network simulated in one process.

## Where the numbers live

`data/*.json`. Every stat, frame, launch value, gadget, chunk polygon, timeline and colour. They are generated by `tools/design_data.py`. Load them. Never copy a number out of a table by hand.

## Your first milestone: M1, the connection test page

Before any game code. One static page on GitHub Pages, under 150 KB, that:

1. Joins a room by three-word code through Trystero.
2. Opens the unordered, no-retransmit channel on Trystero's peer connection and proves it carries data.
3. Runs a 60-second traffic test shaped like the game and reports round trip, jitter, loss.
4. Tests whether a worker timer keeps firing when the tab is hidden.
5. Shows the friendly failure screen when a connection cannot be made.
6. Has a "Copy report" button.

Ace opens it with one friend from different homes. Their pasted reports are the acceptance evidence. Start M1 with the environment report (disk, memory, tool versions). Full criteria: `MASTER_DESIGN.md`, chapter 11.

Then: **M2** the headless simulation with networked bots, **M3** a grey-box that is fun with capsules, **M4** art, **M5** audio, menus and sharing. Do not start M4 until Ace says the grey-box is fun.

## Rules you must never break

1. **Never claim a playtest you did not run.** Every acceptance criterion needs an artefact and the command that made it. Criteria marked H need a human; you may not pass them yourself. If something was skipped, say skipped.
2. **`sim/` imports nothing from the browser.** No DOM, no three.js, no timers, no `Date`, no `Math.random`.
3. **Clients decide nothing but their own inputs.** Hits, launches, KOs, chunk states and spawns come from the host.
4. **One heavy job at a time.** Never Blender, a browser and a test run together.
5. **Every model is thrown or pulled.** Lathe profiles and swept tubes from `ceramic.py`. No primitives, no downloaded meshes.
6. **One material, one texture, no lights.** The packed matcap. No bloom, ever.
7. **Bodies are neutral; saturation is the player's.** Hands are always the player's colour.
8. **Animation timing comes from the frame data.** A pose can never disagree with a hitbox.
9. **Gameplay is identical on every quality tier.**
10. **When you cannot do something well, use the listed fallback and say so.** Chapter 8.6 has one for every risky system. A smaller thing that works beats a larger thing that is claimed.

## What was actually tested in pre-production

On a 2-vCPU, 7 GB box: Rapier 2D at the full body budget (0.65 ms a tick, a whole match in 4.7 s, deterministic); real snapshot sizes (239 to 716 bytes); bundle sizes; headless Chrome software rendering (8.6 ms a frame at 640x360); headless Blender modelling, rendering and glTF export of all four fighters (79 to 96 KB each). Scripts and numbers: `reference/poc/RESULTS.md`.

Not tested: two homes connecting; the unreliable channel; anything on a real GPU; anything that moves or makes a sound. Those are the first things the milestones find out.
